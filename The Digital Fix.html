Doctor Who Christmas special cancelled as Russell T Davies says goodbye

Doctor Who’s Christmas special has been cancelled as Russell T Davies says goodbye and the BBC plans the show’s next era.

Russell T Davies has said goodbye to Doctor Who as the BBC prepares to put the long-running sci-fi series out to competitive tender.

The BBC confirmed the move as part of its plan to secure the future of Doctor Who for new generations. The broadcaster also said the tender fits its Charter and Agreement requirements, while stressing its continued commitment to the series.

The update brings one major disappointment for fans. The previously announced Doctor Who Christmas special will no longer go ahead, following a collective decision by the BBC, Russell T Davies, and Bad Wolf.

Addressing the change directly in a statement shared on Instagram, Davies said "GOODBYE from me to Doctor Who" while also welcoming "a big new future for the show" after the BBC confirmed its tender plans.

He also gave more context around the cancelled festive episode. According to Davies, the Christmas special existed to help protect the show's future during a period of uncertainty. Now the BBC has set out a new route forward, he said there is no need for it.

That means viewers will wait longer for new Doctor Who. Still, Davies framed the delay as part of a wider plan, rather than a retreat from the show. He told fans they would be waiting for more Doctor Who than a single special.

Why the Doctor Who Christmas special was cancelled

Davies also shut down speculation around the scrapped episode. He said there was no script, he never wrote one, and no actor had been approached to play the next Doctor.

That clarification matters because the Christmas special had already sparked questions about what it meant for the show's future. Instead, Davies has drawn a clear line under the idea that a hidden next Doctor plan already existed behind the scenes.

The BBC's own statement strikes a similar long-term tone. Rather than bridge the gap with a one-off episode, the broadcaster wants to invest in the next phase of Doctor Who before the TARDIS returns.

The tender details have not been announced yet. For now, the process sets up a major new chapter for a series that remains one of the BBC's most recognisable shows.

There is still fresh Doctor Who material in production, though. The previously announced animated Doctor Who series for CBeebies remains on the way, so younger viewers still have a new version of the universe ahead.

The BBC also confirmed it retains all Doctor Who IP. BBC Studios will continue to handle global distribution, along with licensing, consumer products, digital projects, and immersive experiences on behalf of the BBC.

Davies, meanwhile, sounded curious about what comes next. For fans, the result feels strange but clear. Doctor Who has no Christmas special on the way, no confirmed next live-action return date, and no announced next Doctor. Yet the BBC says the show remains part of its future, and Davies has left the door open for a stranger, bigger next era.

All we're left with is that Billie Piper crossover and the niggling doubt we'll ever get a resolution to that.

Doctor Who remains available to watch on BBC iPlayer.
