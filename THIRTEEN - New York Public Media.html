Cinema Tropical: Latin American Films on THIRTEEN

September 14, 2026
Listen to audio version of this article
Sponsored by
0:00 / 3:23
Instaread

Don’t miss these films from Mexico and South America, airing Saturday nights on THIRTEEN, the NYC metropolitan-region’s PBS station. Tune-in or livestream at thirteen.org/live for a commercial-free night of film that requires no subscription! See air times, including late-night encores.

From September 15 through October 15, The WNET Group celebrates Hispanic and Latino history and culture with the community’s unique voices in Honoring Our Stories: Hispanic Heritage Month (thirteen.org/HonoringOurStories). Dramatic films; profiles of literary, music, and artistic visionaries; commentaries on identity and history; landmark series from the THIRTEEN Vault; and culinary explorations make up the diverse programming offered for broadcast and streaming audiences.

THIRTEEN Members will be able to use the benefit Passport to stream each film in our Cinema Tropical programming on-demand, on any device and via the PBS and THIRTEEN Explore apps, as well as on this THIRTEEN Specials page.

Learn more about supporting THIRTEEN and the Passport benefit.

Duck Season

September 5, 2026 at 11 p.m.

What could possibly tear two teenage boys away from their video games? In this Mexican comedy, 14-year-olds Flama (Daniel Miranda) and Moko (Diego Catano) meet up for their video game routine on Sunday. But their neighbor Rita and the pizza delivery guy Ulises are going to redirect the course of their day. Duck Season cheekily explores the loneliness of childhood, the effects of divorce and the curious power of love and friendship. The black-and-white film is the winner of numerous awards, including an unprecedented 11 Ariel Awards.

Director Fernando Eimbcke released this film in 2004. Brad Pitt is among the executive producers of his film, Olmo (2025), a family drama set in the 1970s.

Machuca

September 19, 2026 at 10:45 p.m.

Machuca (2004) is set in Chile in 1973 during the final days of President Allende’s life and leading up to Augusto Pinochet’s bloody coup. Pedro (Ariel Mateluna), who comes from modest means, gets a free-ride scholarship to an elite Catholic boarding school run by the forward-thinking Father McEnroe (Ernesto Malbran). Gonzalo (Matías Quer), one of the wealthy boys at the school, quickly takes a shine to Pedro, and the two become fast friends. But as the boys spend more time with one another, their relationship is strained by the turbulent social and political upheaval in Chile. Directed by Andres Wood.

Whisky

September 26, 2026 at 10:56 p.m.

In this Cannes Film Festival award winner from Uruguay, a lonely sock factory owner pretends to be married to to his assistant when his successful younger brother visits — and soon, the couple finds their lives radically altered during a wickedly comical weekend. Whisky — the film title and the word used in place of “cheese!” to provoke smiles while the characters pose for photographs — is directed by Juan Pablo Rebella and Pablo Stoll.

The Amazing Catfish

October 3, 2026 at 10:59 p.m.

What would you have to see in a person in order to trust them with your children? In The Amazing Catfish, two women bond while sharing a hospital room. After their stay, Martha (Lisa Owen), a single mother of four children, lets Claudia (Ximena Ayala) move into her home. Claudia bonds with the family, but Martha’s health slowly deteriorates. The award-winning dramatic film from Mexico, directed by Claudia Sainte-Luce, kicks off our broadcast series of Cinema Tropical films on Saturday nights.
