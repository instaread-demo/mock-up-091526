Bob Mackie, the legendary costume and fashion designer, has died at 87 years old. His death was announced on September 14th, 2026, on his social media. In the death statement, it was shared that Mackie had fulfilled his lifelong dreams of being a fashion designer, and that his work would live on. As of now, his cause of death hasn’t been officially announced. 

According to People, Mackie’s career lasted over six decades, turning him into one of the most recognizable names in entertainment costume design. He collaborated with some of Hollywood’s biggest stars and was known for his sequins, rhinestone, feathers, and statement designs. His looks earned him nine Emmy wins and a Tony Award. 

Mackie’s Beginnings

Mackie’s Hollywood stardom began as a sketch artist in 1961. He sketched for several legendary costume designers, including Edith Head and Jean Louis. The early experience put him ahead of the curve when it came time to create his own name. 

One of his most notable connections was in 1962, while working for Jean Louis. Mackie sketched the sheer, rhinestone-covered gown Marilyn Monroe wore when she sang “Happy Birthday, Mr. President” to John F. Kennedy. 

Television also became another facet of Mackie’s career. After working with Mitzi Gaynor, Mackie was hired to design the wardrobe for The Carol Burnett Show. He stayed with the series for its entire run. 

Unforgettable Looks

Mackie had so many looks over the years, but a few were unforgettable. One such piece was Carol Burnett’s famous curtain dress from The Carol Burnett Show. The dress was styled for the 1976 “Went with the Wind” sketch, a parody of Gone with the Wind. 

Another superstar connection was Cher. 

Mackie’s partnership with the singer was one of his closest relationships. He designed several of her most stunning television, concert, and red-carpet looks, including the 1986 Academy Awards outfit.

The Associated Press also pointed out that Mackie dressed Diana Ross, Tina Turner, Barbra Streisand, Madonna, and many others. Mackie’s designs were more than just clothes. They created an identity for every celebrity.

Legacy Beyond Sequins

Mentioned above, Mackie won several awards for his work. He also received three Academy Award nominations. The stylist was later inducted into the Television Academy Hall of Fame, and later created collectible Barbie dolls and ready-to-wear fashion.

His influence has only grown over the years, with contemporary stars like Zendaya, Miley Cyrus, and Taylor Swift all donning his archival designs to premier events. 

For generations of fashion and music fans, Bob Mackie’s designs were just as important as the songs and stars who wore them.
