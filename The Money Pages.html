Savings rates rising as choice of accounts hits record high

By Kate Saines in Saving & Banking, Saving & Banking News Posted on September 10, 2026 | Modified on September 10, 2026 0

Savings rates are rising and there is ‘record choice’ of accounts available to customers, new analysis by Moneyfacts has revealed today.

It has documented the highest number of non-ISA savings products on record – with 1,894 accounts for savers to choose from.

Even when ISAs are considered in the mix, the 2,642 accounts available offer an impressive selection for savers. What’s more the number of savings providers in the market has risen to 160 – the highest figure on record.

And in more good news for savers, interest being applied to fixed-rate savings accounts is also on the rise.

The typical rate on a one-year fixed rate account has jumped to 4.28% – the highest since October 2024.

For longer-term fixed rate deals, the typical rate has also climbed to 4.31% – the highest since January 2024.

In the ISA market, fixed rate deals are also on the up with the average fixed rate one year ISA rising to 4.26% – the highest since September 2024 – and the typical longer-term rate went up to 4.3%.

Fixed rate accounts aren’t for everyone. Customers typically require a lump sum, and this must be locked away for the relevant time period to earn interest.

For anyone looking for a more flexible option, average easy access rates remained unchanged, according to Moneyfacts, at 2.53% for the third month running.

Meanwhile, the Moneyfacts Average New Savings Rate has risen for the seventh time running to 3.62%. Over the past year the rate has risen from 3.46% to 3.62%.

Caitlyn Eastell, personal finance analyst at Moneyfacts, said with savings choice at record levels, it’s easier than ever for savers to find better homes for their cash and protect their returns.

“Savers have been handed another welcome boost this month,” she said, “with fixed savings rates continuing to climb and competition remaining incredibly strong across the market.”

She added: “This continued upward movement is positive news for savers who are looking to secure a guaranteed return, particularly those who have been waiting to see if rates could climb even higher.

“It also means that savers who locked away their cash some time ago could now find significantly more competitive options available.”
